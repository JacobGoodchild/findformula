FORMULA 1 — Added mass of a ball touching a spherical tank (ideal fluid)
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: derived exactly + verified numerically.
NOVELTY UPDATE: probably a minor/routine extension. The viscous analogue (Formula 2) was
published by Papavassiliou & Alexander (J. Fluid Mech. 2017, arXiv:1602.06912), and the
external two-sphere potential-flow case was treated by Small & Weihs (1975). The inside-
touching closed form has not been found in print, but an expert would likely call it an exercise.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  When a ball speeds up inside a fluid, it has to push fluid out of the way, so it
  feels heavier than it is. That extra effective mass is the "added mass". For a ball
  alone in open water it is exactly 1/2 the mass of the water the ball displaces.
  What is it when the ball is sitting against the inside wall of a closed spherical
  tank full of fluid, and moves along the line through the two centres?

SET-UP
  Ball radius a, spherical tank radius b (b > a), the ball touches the tank wall from
  inside. The ball moves along the line of centres; the tank is fixed. Fluid is ideal
  (inviscid, incompressible, irrotational: "potential flow").
  Added mass  M = C * rho * (4/3) * pi * a^3   (rho = fluid density).

THE FORMULA
  Let r = a/b  (0 < r < 1).  Then

      C(r) = (3/2) * SUM_{k=0..inf} 1 / (1 + k(1-r))^3  -  1

           = (3/2) * q^3 * zeta(3, q)  -  1,      q = 1/(1-r) = b/(b-a),

  where zeta(s, q) is the Hurwitz zeta function.

UNIFIED VERSION (same derivation; the fixed surface can be any sphere or a plane)
  Let the ball (radius a) touch a fixed sphere of SIGNED radius b:
     b > 0 : a container, the ball touches it from inside
     b = inf : a flat wall
     b < 0 : an external fixed ball of radius |b|, the two touch from outside.
  With lambda = 1 - a/b:
      C = (3/2) * lambda^(-3) * zeta(3, 1/lambda) - 1 = (3/2) * SUM_{k>=0} (1 + k*lambda)^(-3) - 1

  Special cases:
     flat wall (lambda = 1):            C = (3/2) zeta(3) - 1 = 0.8030853...   (known result, reproduced)
     tank twice the ball's size (r=1/2): C = 12 (zeta(3) - 1) - 1 = 1.4246828...
     two equal balls touching (lambda=2): C = (21/16) zeta(3) - 1 = 0.5776997...
     r -> 1 (ball fills the tank):       C -> infinity (fluid is trapped; makes physical sense)

HOW IT WAS DERIVED
  Method of images (Hicks 1880 / Butler's sphere theorem). The image of an axial point
  dipole in a rigid sphere is a single axial dipole at the inverse point, strength
  scaled by -(R/f)^3. Bouncing between the two touching spheres produces an infinite
  chain of images. Inverting about the contact point turns both spheres into parallel
  planes, so the image positions form an arithmetic progression. The strength ratios
  then telescope exactly:
      A_n / A_{n-1} = ((n(b-a) + a) / (n(b-a) + b))^3   =>   A_k = A_0 * b^3/(a + k(b-a))^3.
  The added mass is -(rho/U) * (surface integral of phi * n_x over the ball), which equals
  (4 pi/3)(sum of image dipoles inside the ball) + (ball volume)(x-gradient of the images
  outside it, evaluated at the ball's centre). Both sums are Hurwitz zeta values, and
  together they give the formula above.
  (Symbolic algebra: physics/images.py)

HOW IT WAS VERIFIED (not just trusted)
  1. Reproduces the known flat-wall value (3/2)zeta(3) - 1 = 0.80309, which was also
     obtained independently by solving the problem as a differential equation in
     tangent-sphere coordinates (physics/tangent_wall.py: 0.7527, 0.7957, 0.8021, 0.80297
     -> 0.80309 as the cut-off is refined).
  2. Built the actual flow field from the images and checked the physics at 2000 random
     points on each surface (physics/verify_images.py, physics/verify_general.py):
       - no flow through the tank wall: error ~1e-8 .. 1e-15
       - fluid moves with the ball on its surface: error -> 0 as more images are added
     With both boundary conditions met, the solution is the unique correct one.
  3. Computed the added mass by brute-force surface integration (no zeta functions used)
     and compared it with the formula:
       a=1, b=2  : 1.424682623 (N=6000 images)  vs formula 1.424682838
       a=1, b=5  : 0.944931366                  vs 0.944931549
       a=1, b=-1 : 0.577699652                  vs 0.577699685
       a=1, b=-3 : 0.677786230                  vs 0.677786291
       a=2, b=-1 : 0.531170046                  vs 0.531170067
     The small gap shrinks by ~4x each time the number of images doubles
     (2.7e-6 -> 6.7e-7 -> 2.2e-7), i.e. it is truncation error converging onto the formula.

NOVELTY
  Web searches (Sept 2026) found no statement of this closed form for a ball touching
  the inside of a spherical container. The flat-wall case (3/2)zeta(3) - 1 is known.
  The external two-ball case may already exist in 19th/20th-century hydrodynamics
  literature (Hicks, Basset, Lamb), since it uses the same classical technique.
  Direct access to arXiv and OEIS was blocked from this environment, so treat
  "new" as "not found", not as certain.

WHY IT MATTERS
  Gives an exact benchmark for numerical (boundary-element / CFD) codes in a near-contact
  geometry, which is notoriously hard to resolve numerically. It also gives the effective
  inertia of a body resting against the wall of a spherical fluid-filled tank (bearings,
  sloshing/fluid-structure models, particles in droplets or cavities).

FORMULA 2 — Viscous torque on a spinning ball touching the inside of a spherical tank
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: derived exactly + verified numerically (independent stress
integration).
NOVELTY UPDATE: NOT NEW. The same Hurwitz-zeta formula (inside and outside versions) is in
Papavassiliou & Alexander, "Exact solutions for hydrodynamic interactions of two squirming
spheres", J. Fluid Mech. (2017), eqs. (11)-(12); arXiv:1602.06912. Kept here as a rediscovery.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  A ball sits against the inside wall of a closed spherical tank filled with a very
  viscous fluid (think honey; "Stokes flow"). The ball spins slowly about the line
  through the two centres. How much twisting force (torque) does the fluid resist with?

THE FORMULA
  Ball radius a, tank radius b, r = a/b, q = 1/(1-r) = b/(b-a), viscosity mu,
  spin rate Omega:

      Torque = 8*pi*mu*Omega*a^3 * SUM_{k=0..inf} 1/(1 + k(1-r))^3
             = 8*pi*mu*Omega*a^3 * q^3 * zeta(3, q)          (Hurwitz zeta)

  (8*pi*mu*Omega*a^3 is the torque on the same ball spinning in unbounded fluid.)

  Unified form: fixed sphere of signed radius b (b>0 container, b=inf wall, b<0 external
  ball touching from outside), lambda = 1 - a/b:
      Torque = 8*pi*mu*Omega*a^3 * lambda^(-3) * zeta(3, 1/lambda)

  Special cases:
     flat wall:                     8 pi mu Omega a^3 * zeta(3)        (Jeffery 1915, known, reproduced)
     tank of twice the ball radius: 8 pi mu Omega a^3 * 8(zeta(3)-1) = 1.6164552... x unbounded value
     equal external fixed ball:     8 pi mu Omega a^3 * (7/8) zeta(3)  (may be known classically)

DERIVATION
  Azimuthal (swirling) Stokes flow can be built exactly from point "rotlets" on the axis.
  A rotlet of strength G at distance f from the centre of a no-slip sphere of radius R is
  cancelled on that sphere by a rotlet of strength -G(R/f)^3 at the inverse point. This is
  exact: for axial rotlets the velocity is purely azimuthal, u_phi = G*rho/d^3, and on a
  sphere the distance ratio to a point and its inverse point is constant. The chain of
  images between the two touching spheres has the same arithmetic-progression geometry
  as in Formula 1, so the strengths telescope to G_k = Omega a^3/(1 + k(1-r))^3. The torque
  on the ball is 8*pi*mu times the total rotlet strength inside it. (Images outside the
  ball give a flow that is regular inside the ball, so they exert no net torque.)

VERIFICATION (physics/verify_torque.py)
  Flow built from 6000 images; no-slip checked at 3000 random points per surface
  (errors 1e-9 .. 1e-25). The torque was then computed independently by numerically
  integrating the viscous shear stress over the ball's surface:
       case            numeric       formula
       wall (b=1e9)    1.20205685    1.20205690  (= zeta(3))
       a=1, b=2        1.61645482    1.61645523
       a=1, b=5        1.29662095    1.29662103
       a=1, b=-1       1.05179979    1.05179979  (= 7/8 zeta(3))
       a=1, b=-3       1.11852418    1.11852419
  (Numbers are torque / (8 pi mu Omega a^3). Remaining gaps are image-truncation error.)

NOVELTY
  Rotating eccentric spheres are studied in the literature (e.g. series solutions in
  bispherical coordinates, including slip variants), and Jeffery (1915) has the wall
  case. I found no explicit Hurwitz-zeta closed form for the internally touching case.
  With arXiv/OEIS blocked, "not found" is the honest status.

FORMULA 3 — How much a 3D (and 4D) quantum walker stays "stuck" at its starting point
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: derived exactly, checked by two independent computations.
Novelty: the 2D case is known (Inui, Konishi, Konno, Phys. Rev. A 69, 052323 (2004)); the
2D values reproduced here match theirs exactly. I found no
statement of the 3D/4D closed forms or the all-dimensions integral, but I could not open
several papers (IOP/arXiv blocked), so "not found" is the honest status.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  A quantum walk is the quantum version of a random walk. A classical random walker on a
  3D grid wanders off and the chance of finding it back at the start dies away to zero.
  The "Grover" quantum walk, a standard model in quantum computing, behaves differently:
  part of the wave stays trapped near the start forever. What exactly is the long-run
  chance of finding the walker back at its starting point?

MODEL
  Grover walk on the d-dimensional grid Z^d, 2d coin states (one per direction +x,-x,...),
  Grover coin G = (1/d)J - I, moving shift. Start: at the origin with coin state phi.
  pbar(phi) = long-time average of Prob(walker at origin).

THE FORMULA
  Let  A_d = INTEGRAL_0^inf erfc(sqrt(s))^d ds  (equivalently E[1/(d + t_1^2+...+t_d^2)]
  for independent standard Cauchy variables t_i), and B_d = (1 - d*A_d)/d. Then

      pbar(phi) = 2 * [ (d*A_d)^2 * |<u|phi>|^2  +  B_d^2 * ||P_anti phi||^2 ]

  where u = (1,...,1)/sqrt(2d) is the uniform coin state and P_anti projects onto the
  d states e_{+i} - e_{-i} (normalised). Coin states orthogonal to both never get stuck.

  Exact values of A_d:
      d = 1 :  1/2
      d = 2 :  1/2 - 1/pi                          (B_2 = 1/pi)
      d = 3 :  1/2 - (3 - sqrt(3))/pi              = 0.0963992368704200348980...
      d = 4 :  1/2 - (18 - 8*sqrt(3))/(3*pi)       = 0.0603510706887014361369...
      d = 5 :  1/2 - 10/pi + 40 sqrt(3)/(3 pi) - 20 sqrt(3) arccos(1/4)/pi^2
               = 0.0415386564485422292429170321891...   [added later the same night: PSLQ at
               80 digits, confirmed to 1e-80. Why it closes: A_d = (1/2) E[min Z_k^2] is a
               moment over a region of the sphere S^(d-1), and on the even sphere S^4 such
               integrals reduce to lower-dimensional angles. For d = 6 (odd sphere S^5) no
               closed form was found. By min/max inclusion-exclusion the same result says
               E[max of 5 squared standard normals] = 1 + 20 sqrt3/pi - 40 sqrt3 arccos(1/4)/pi^2
               = 2.77374906709428422583...; this is the mean-square max of 5 half-normals, which
               S. Finch (arXiv:1112.0499, "Mean width of a regular cross-polytope") appears to treat
               ("mean square counterparts", n <= 6), so the integral itself is PROBABLY KNOWN.
               The new part is only its use here: 5D trapping, start +x,
               pbar = 5A^2 + B^2 = 0.03373729729788322185391...; uniform start 50A^2 = 0.08627299897750094626...]

  Headline 3D numbers:
      start in one direction (e.g. +x):  pbar = 3A^2 + B^2 = 0.0840162046742951524...
      start in the uniform coin state:   pbar = 18 A^2     = 0.1672706316455882907...
                                        (the most "sticky" possible start)
      At even times the return probability itself tends to 2*pbar; at odd times to 0.

DERIVATION (sketch)
  In momentum space the walk operator S(k)G has eigenvalues +1 and -1 for every k (flat
  bands); these cause the trapping. Solving G psi = lambda S(k)^* psi gives the flat-band
  eigenvector v_j = 1/(1 + lambda*conj(s_j)). For lambda = +1 its components are
  (1 +/- i*tan(k_i/2))/2, and for lambda = -1 they are (1 -/+ i*cot(k_i/2))/2. When k_i is
  uniform on the circle, tan(k_i/2) and cot(k_i/2) are standard Cauchy variables, so the
  momentum-averaged projector becomes
      M = (A/2) J + (B/2) * blockdiag([[1,-1],[-1,1]]),
  with A = E[1/(d + sum t_i^2)]. Writing 1/x = INTEGRAL e^{-sx} ds and using
  E[e^{-s t^2}] = e^s erfc(sqrt s) gives A_d = INTEGRAL erfc(sqrt s)^d ds. Then
  pbar = 2 ||M phi||^2, and the +1 and -1 bands give equal contributions. The closed
  forms were found by integer-relation search (PSLQ) at 60 digits with small
  coefficients, e.g. 6*A_4 - 3 + 36/pi - 16*sqrt(3)/pi = 0.

VERIFICATION
  1. Brute-force simulation of the actual walk (quantum/sim_grover.py):
       3D, start +x: predicted P(return, even t -> inf) = 0.168032;
                     simulated t=68: 0.168233, t=70: 0.167271 (oscillating in towards it)
       2D, start +x: predicted 0.334688; simulated t=398: 0.334106, t=400: 0.335266
  2. Numerical diagonalisation of the walk at 64^3 momenta, averaging the +1 projector
     (quantum/torus_check.py): agrees with the matrix M above to 2e-7 (grid error).
  3. The 3D and 4D closed forms agree with the integral to 60 digits (quantum/grover_A.py).

FORMULA 4 — Trapping in the "lazy" (lackadaisical) Grover quantum walk, 2D and 3D,
            as an exact function of the laziness weight l
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: derived exactly, checked against the integrals (30-40 digits)
and against brute-force simulations of the walk.
Novelty: the lackadaisical walk is an active topic (Wong 2015 onwards). A Sept-2026 arXiv
paper (2609.08973) studies flat-band localization for the 1D version. I found no exact
2D/3D trapping formula, but that is "not found", not certainty.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  Same quantum walker as Formula 3, but at every step it may also "stay put" through a
  self-loop with adjustable weight l (a knob used in quantum search algorithms). Laziness
  removes one of the two trapping mechanisms, so the return probability no longer flickers
  between even and odd times; it settles to a single value. What is that value, as a
  function of l?

MODEL
  Z^d, coin space = 2d moving directions + 1 self-loop. Coin G = 2|s><s| - I with
  |s> = (1,...,1, sqrt(l)) / sqrt(2d + l). Moving shift. Start at the origin.

GENERAL STRUCTURE (any d)
  a(l) = (1/2) * INTEGRAL_0^inf exp(-l s/2) * erfc(sqrt s)^d ds,    b = (1 - (2d+l) a)/(2d)
  Long-time return probability:
     start in the self-loop state:    P = l (2d + l) a^2
     start moving in one direction:   P = 2d a^2 + 2 b^2 + l a^2
  (The flat band at eigenvalue +1 survives the self-loop; the one at -1 does not.)

EXACT CLOSED FORMS
  d = 2:   a(l) = (1/l) * [ 1 - (4/pi) * arctan(q)/q ],        q = sqrt(1 + l/2)

  d = 3:   a(l) = (1/l) * [ 1 - 3 sqrt(2/(l+2)) * ( 1 + (2/pi) * ( arcsin(2/(l+4))
                                                  - 2 arcsin( sqrt(2/(l+4)) ) ) ) ]

  (As l -> 0 these tend to half the non-lazy values A_2/2 and A_3/2 from Formula 3.)

  Key lemma used for d = 3 (proved via a trivariate Gaussian orthant probability):
     INTEGRAL_0^inf e^{-b x^2} erfc(x)^2 dx
        = (1/2) sqrt(pi/b) * [ 1 + (2/pi)( arcsin(1/(b+1)) - 2 arcsin(1/sqrt(b+1)) ) ]
  Checked against numerical integration to ~40 digits at b = 1/3, 7.

VERIFICATION
  Closed forms vs direct numerical integration: agree to 30-40 digits for several l.
  Simulations of the actual walk (time-averaged return probability, late times):
     3D, l=1, start self-loop : simulated 0.013821   formula 0.013729
     3D, l=1, start +x        : simulated 0.040175   formula 0.040179
     3D, l=4, start self-loop : simulated 0.051919   formula 0.051862
     3D, l=4, start +x        : simulated 0.035698   formula 0.035716
     2D, l=1, start self-loop : simulated 0.031086   formula 0.031077
     2D, l=1, start +x        : simulated 0.076954   formula 0.076953
  The 3D simulation reaches only t=90 (memory limit), and its error oscillates and decays
  like t^(-3/2) (quantum/lazy_trend.py), as expected from the escaping part of the wave.
  Code: quantum/lazy.py, quantum/lazy_avg.py, quantum/lazy2d.py

FORMULA 5 — Trapping of the FLIP-FLOP Grover walk on 3D crystal lattices (simple cubic
            and body-centred cubic), in closed form via Watson's lattice integrals
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: derived exactly, integer relations found at 40-70 digits,
checked against brute-force simulations of both walks.
Novelty: trapping in flip-flop Grover walks is known to exist (and is computed in 2D;
Higuchi-Konno-Sato-Segawa give general-d integral descriptions). I found no exact 3D
values, and no link to Watson's integrals. "Not found", not certainty.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  The "flip-flop" Grover walk is the version used in most quantum-search algorithms: after
  each hop the walker's internal arrow flips to point back where it came from. On a 3D
  crystal, what fraction of the walker stays trapped at the starting site forever?

GENERAL RESULT (any lattice whose neighbour set is +/-delta_1, ..., +/-delta_P)
  Long-time average return probability, starting in coin state phi:
      pbar(phi) = ||M_+ phi||^2 + ||M_- phi||^2,     M_+ = I/2 - N
  with N_{(s,p),(t,q)} = (1/4) [ G(0) - G(delta_p) - G(delta_q) + G(t*delta_q - s*delta_p) ]
  where G(x) = E_k[ cos(k.x) / sum_r (1 - cos k.delta_r) ] is the lattice Green function.
  For bipartite lattices (SC, BCC), M_- = M_+, so pbar = 2 ||M phi||^2.
  Derivation: the flat bands of U = S(2|u><u| - I) live in K = {psi : <u,psi> = 0 = <u,S psi>},
  where U = -S. So the +1 flat projector is (I-S)/2 - (I-S)uu^*(I-S)/(2<u,(I-S)u>);
  averaging it over k gives the Green-function entries above.

SIMPLE CUBIC (6 coin states)
  Watson's integral  W = sqrt(6)/(32 pi^3) * Gamma(1/24) Gamma(5/24) Gamma(7/24) Gamma(11/24)
                       = 1.5163860591519780181560...
  M = I/2 - N,  N_diag = 1/6,
      N(+i,-i)  = alpha = ( 7W + 54/(pi^2 W) - 12 ) / 36
      N(i, j!=i) = beta  = ( 24 - 7W - 54/(pi^2 W) ) / 144      (note alpha + 4 beta = 1/3)
  Start in one coin state (e.g. +x):
      pbar = 2 [ 1/9 + alpha^2 + 4 beta^2 ] = 0.26672720352085343375733992...
  Start in the uniform coin state: pbar = 0 (it escapes completely).

BODY-CENTRED CUBIC (8 coin states, neighbours (+-1,+-1,+-1))
  W_b = Gamma(1/4)^4 / (4 pi^3) = 1.3932039296856768591842...
  N_diag = 1/8,
      same pair, opposite arrow:          alpha   = ( 3 W_b + 36/(pi^2 W_b) - 6 ) / 16
      other pair, difference (2,0,0)-type: beta_s = ( 2 - W_b + 4/(pi^2 W_b) ) / 16
      other pair, difference (2,2,0)-type: beta_d = ( 1 - 8/(pi^2 W_b) ) / 8
  (row sum 1/8 + alpha + 3 beta_s + 3 beta_d = 1/2 exactly)
  Start in one coin state:
      pbar = 2 [ 9/64 + alpha^2 + 3 beta_s^2 + 3 beta_d^2 ] = 0.32150479124143771009523...

VERIFICATION
  - Integer relations found by PSLQ at 40 digits (SC re-checked at 70 digits: identical
    relations). Lattice Green functions were computed two independent ways: Bessel
    integrals for SC and accelerated exact binomial series for BCC. The BCC series
    reproduces Watson's BCC constant to 40 digits.
  - Brute-force simulation of each actual walk (quantum/sim_flipflop.py, quantum/sim_bcc.py):
       SC,  start +x: simulated time-average 0.266687 vs formula 0.266727
       BCC, start one arrow: simulated 0.321756 vs formula 0.321505
    (Simulations are limited to t <= 80 by memory; the escaping part decays like t^-3/2.)
  Code: quantum/flipflop.py, quantum/bcc_lgf.py, quantum/bcc_trap.py

FORMULA 6 — Trapping of the flip-flop Grover walk on the FACE-CENTRED CUBIC lattice,
            and a far-edge FCC lattice Green function governed by the SIMPLE-CUBIC
            Watson constant
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: closed forms found by PSLQ (32-50 digits), confirmed by exact
lattice equations (residual 1e-42) and a brute-force simulation of the walk.
Novelty: not found in the literature (same caveats as Formulas 3-5).
==========================================================================================

WHY FCC IS DIFFERENT
  FCC (the crystal structure of copper, aluminium and gold) is not bipartite. So the two
  trapping channels (eigenvalues +1 and -1) differ. The -1 channel needs the lattice
  Green function at the opposite edge of the band:
      G'(x) = E_k[ cos(k.x) / (6 + 2s) ],   s = c1c2 + c2c3 + c3c1,  c_i = cos k_i.

A SIDE RESULT: FCC far-edge Green function = simple-cubic Watson constant
  G'(0) = E[1/(6 + 2s)] = W/(6 sqrt 2)
        = sqrt(3) Gamma(1/24)Gamma(5/24)Gamma(7/24)Gamma(11/24) / (192 pi^3)
        = 0.17870781088718480526196852593401317642...
  where W is the SIMPLE-CUBIC Watson integral (Formula 5). Equivalently,
  E[1/(3 + c1c2 + c2c3 + c3c1)] = W/(3 sqrt 2).
  (Joyce, "Some exact results for the face-centred cubic lattice Green function" (2011)
  and "On the exact evaluation of the FCC lattice Green function", J. Stat. Phys. (2011),
  treats the FCC Green function outside the band using elliptic integrals and singular
  values. This identity very likely follows from that work and may be stated there.
  Treat it as probably known. The NEW part of Formula 6 is the application to
  quantum-walk trapping.)
  Further values, Y = sqrt(2) W:
      G'(1,1,0) = (2 - Y)/12
      G'(2,0,0) = (7Y + 108/(pi^2 Y) - 36 sqrt(3)/pi) / 72
      G'(2,1,1) = (2Y - 3 - 27/(pi^2 Y)) / 9
      G'(2,2,0) = (Y + 54/(pi^2 Y) + 6 sqrt(3)/pi - 8) / 6
  These satisfy the FCC lattice equations 6G'(x) + (1/2) sum_nbrs G'(x+e) = delta_{x,0}
  exactly (numerical residual 3e-42).
  Ordinary FCC Green-function differences (FCC Watson constant
  W_f = 3 Gamma(1/3)^6 / (2^(14/3) pi^4)), consistent with the classical theory:
      G(0)-G(2,0,0) = (4W_f - 3/(pi^2 W_f))/6,  G(0)-G(2,1,1) = (2W_f + 3/(pi^2 W_f) - 1)/3,
      G(0)-G(2,2,0) = (8 - 12 W_f - 9/(pi^2 W_f))/3.

THE TRAPPING FORMULA
  12 coin states (+/-delta_p, p = 1..6). With y = t*delta_q - s*delta_p:
      M_+ = I/2 - N_+,   N_+[(s,p),(t,q)] = (1/3 - [G(0) - G(y)]) / 4
      M_- = I/2 - N_-,   N_-[(s,p),(t,q)] = (G'(0) + 2G'(delta) + G'(y)) / 4
      pbar(phi) = ||M_+ phi||^2 + ||M_- phi||^2
  Start in one coin state:
      pbar = 0.37891463105431168342611113503903...
      (+1 channel 0.18948881307124000416..., -1 channel 0.18942581798307167926...)
  Every entry is an explicit combination of 1, sqrt(3)/pi, W_f, 1/(pi^2 W_f), Y and
  1/(pi^2 Y) (code: quantum/fcc_final.py).

VERIFICATION
  - Green-function values computed by exact k3-integration plus 2D quadrature, at 32 and
    50 digits; PSLQ relations are stable and have small coefficients.
  - The G' closed forms satisfy the lattice equations to 1e-42.
  - Brute-force simulation of the FCC flip-flop walk (quantum/sim_fcc.py):
    time-average 0.379379 vs formula 0.378915 (simulation limited to t = 56).

FORMULA 7 — Flip-flop Grover walk on the TRIANGULAR lattice: exact trapping probability
            (plus a general "electrical network" form of flip-flop trapping)
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: closed form; lattice equations satisfied exactly; simulation
agrees to 6 digits.  Novelty: not found in the literature.
==========================================================================================

GENERAL PRINCIPLE (holds for any periodic graph)
  For the flip-flop Grover walk, the +1 flat-band projector is the projector onto the
  cycle space: I minus the Kirchhoff "transfer-current" matrix Y of the lattice (the
  same matrix that gives edge probabilities in uniform spanning trees). The -1 flat band
  is the same with the signless Laplacian D + A in place of the Laplacian D - A.
  So, starting on arc e at the origin:
      pbar(e) = (1/4) * SUM_{arcs f at the origin} [ (delta_ef - Y(e,f))^2
                                                  + (delta_ef - Y_signless(e,f))^2 ]
  (This is how Formulas 5 and 6 arise; for bipartite lattices both terms are equal.)

TRIANGULAR LATTICE (6 coin states)
  Needed Green-function values. Laplacian potential kernel (classical resistances):
      a(1) = 1/3,  a(sqrt3) = 2 sqrt3/pi - 2/3,  a(2) = 8/3 - 4 sqrt3/pi
  Signless Laplacian (far band edge; these appear to be new closed forms):
      X := G'(0) = E[ 1/(3 + cos k1 + cos k2 + cos(k1-k2)) ] = 2^(2/3) Gamma(1/3)^3 / (8 pi^2)
                 = 0.38653175565464278858999735052334...
      G'(1)      = (1 - 3X)/3
      G'(sqrt3)  = (9X - 2 - sqrt3/(pi X)) / 3
      G'(2)      = (3X - 4 + 2 sqrt3/(pi X)) / 3
  THE RESULT, starting in one coin state:
      +1 channel = (43 pi^2 - 120 sqrt3 pi + 324) / (72 pi^2)
      -1 channel = X^2 - X/2 + 19/72 - sqrt3/(6 pi) - sqrt3/(18 pi X) + 1/(8 pi^2 X^2)
      pbar = X^2 - X/2 + 31/36 - 11 sqrt3/(6 pi) + 9/(2 pi^2) - sqrt3/(18 pi X) + 1/(8 pi^2 X^2)
           = 0.26795570253708055129404796163749...

VERIFICATION
  - Green functions computed to 45 digits (exact k2 integration + tanh-sinh quadrature);
    PSLQ relations have small integer coefficients; G'(0) matches the Gamma form to 1e-46.
  - The closed forms satisfy the triangular-lattice equations 3G'(x) + (1/2) SUM_nbrs
    G'(x+e) = delta_{x,0} exactly.
  - Brute-force simulation of the actual walk (quantum2d/sim2d.py), time average over
    t = 201..500: 0.2679596 vs formula 0.2679557.
  - Same code on the square lattice gives 0.16734378 (simulation 0.16734263). That is the
    classical 2D case, which simplifies to pbar = 1/2 - 2/pi + 3/pi^2 (from a(1,1) = 2/pi,
    a(2,0) = 2 - 4/pi); very likely already known.
  Code: quantum2d/lgf2d.py, flipflop2d.py, id_tri.py, tri_final.py, tri_sym.py, sim2d.py

FORMULA 8 — Flip-flop Grover walk on the KAGOME lattice (and honeycomb): exact trapping,
            including the extra trapping caused by the kagome flat band
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: all components identified by PSLQ (35 digits, small integer
coefficients) and cross-checked by Brillouin-zone diagonalisation and simulation.
Novelty: not found in the literature.
==========================================================================================

KEY NEW INGREDIENT
  On kagome the ordinary random walk has a flat band (eigenvalue -1/2). Through the
  Szegedy correspondence this creates two EXTRA flat eigenvalues of the quantum walk,
  lambda = e^{+-2 pi i/3}, so a kagome walker is trapped through four channels, not two:
      <b|Pi_lambda|a> = (1/6) [ F(o,o) - lambda F(h_b,o) - conj(lambda) F(o,h_a) + F(h_b,h_a) ]
  where F is the real-space projector onto the kagome flat band and h_a is the head of
  arc a. The flat-band null vector is v(k) = (1/z2 - 1/z1, 1 - 1/z2, 1/z1 - 1), and
  |v|^2 is proportional to the triangular dispersion. So F reduces to triangular-lattice
  resistances.

AN ELLIPTIC-INTEGRAL EVALUATION USED
  E_k[ 1/(11 - cos k1 - cos k2 - cos(k1-k2)) ] = K(k) / (4 sqrt(2) pi),   k^2 = 5/32
  (the triangular lattice Green function at energy 11, i.e. the honeycomb one at energy 5).
  Checked to 40 digits. Classical Horiguchi-type formulas probably imply it.

KAGOME RESULT (4 coin states per site, start in one coin state; K = K(k), E = E(k), k^2 = 5/32)
  Transfer currents (Laplacian):         Y  = ( 1/2, 1/4, (1 + 6 sqrt3/pi)/36, (4 - 3 sqrt3/pi)/18 )
  Signless-Laplacian transfer currents:  YQ = ( 1/2, (5 - 5 sqrt2 K/pi)/12,
                                              (24 sqrt2 E/pi - 4 sqrt2 K/pi - 11)/12,
                                              (4 + 3 sqrt2 K/pi - 8 sqrt2 E/pi)/4 )
  Channel contributions:
     +1 flat band :  (235 pi^2 - 42 sqrt3 pi + 108) / (2592 pi^2)       = 0.0859517261673060...
     -1 flat band :  163/288 - 23 sqrt2 E/(12 pi) + 127 sqrt2 K/(288 pi)
                     + 4E^2/pi^2 - 13 K E/(6 pi^2) + 61 K^2/(144 pi^2)   = 0.0845635611617672...
     e^{+-2pi i/3} (each):  (19 - 42 sqrt3/pi + 108/pi^2) / 648           = 0.0104735713358908...
  TOTAL long-time average return probability:
     pbar = 103/144 - 7 sqrt3/(48 pi) + 3/(8 pi^2) - 23 sqrt2 E/(12 pi) + 127 sqrt2 K/(288 pi)
            + 4E^2/pi^2 - 13 K E/(6 pi^2) + 61 K^2/(144 pi^2)
          = 0.19146243000085471390403...

HONEYCOMB (bipartite, 3 coin states): the transfer currents are forced to be (2/3, 1/6, 1/6),
  so pbar = 2 * (1/4)[(1/3)^2 + 2(1/6)^2] = 1/12 exactly. (Simulation: 0.083337.)
  This is simple enough that it may already be known (see the 2015 honeycomb-network paper).

VERIFICATION
  - Row sums of the transfer-current matrices equal 1 (current conservation).
  - Independent numerical diagonalisation of the 12x12 Bloch walk operator on a 90x90
    momentum grid, averaging each flat-band projector: +1: 0.0859501, -1: 0.0845636,
    e^{+-2pi i/3}: 0.0104728 each; total 0.191459 (grid error ~1e-6).
  - Brute-force simulation of the kagome walk: time-average over t = 151..400 is 0.19099
    (still decaying toward the limit; the escaping part falls off like 1/t in 2D).
  Code: quantum2d/engine.py, honey_kagome.py, kag_flat.py, kag_all.py, kag_sym.py,
        kag_bands.py, simgraph.py

FORMULA 9 — Exact flip-flop Grover-walk trapping on four more 2D lattices:
            LIEB, DICE (T3), TRUNCATED SQUARE (4.8.8) and CHECKERBOARD (planar pyrochlore)

value confirmed by an independent Bloch-diagonalisation grid check (lattices/gridcheck.py)
and by direct simulation.  Novelty: not found in the literature.
==========================================================================================

METHOD (general engine, any 2D periodic graph)
  Trapping = sum over flat eigenvalues of the walk: +1 (Laplacian transfer currents), -1
  (signless-Laplacian transfer currents), plus mu +- i sqrt(1-mu^2) for every flat band mu
  of the random walk D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2} (weights 1/sqrt(deg), prefactor 1/(2(1-mu^2))).
  Brillouin-zone averages are done by an exact residue sum in k2 and quadrature in k1.
  pbar(start arc) = long-time average probability of being back at the start vertex.

LIEB lattice (corner sites deg 4, edge-centre sites deg 2; flat band mu = 0 -> lambda = +-i)
  start at a corner site:   pbar = (1 - 4/pi + 6/pi^2)/4 = 0.0836718892797159856...
                            (+1 and -1 channels (1 - 4/pi + 6/pi^2)/16 each; flat channels
                            (1 - 4/pi + 6/pi^2)/8)  -- exactly half the square-lattice value
  start at an edge site:    pbar = 1/8 exactly   (channels 1/32, 1/32, 1/16)
  grid check: 0.08367189, 0.125;  simulation: 0.083685, 0.125017

                             flat channels (4 - 4 sqrt3/pi + 9/pi^2)/144)
  start at a rim site:      pbar = 11/48 exactly   (channels 3/32, 3/32, 1/24)

TRUNCATED SQUARE 4.8.8 (bipartite, deg 3).  Let  c = sqrt(2) * arccos(1/3) / pi = 0.5541...
  Edge resistances: square edge (2 + c)/4, octagon-octagon bridge 1 - c/2 (Foster's sum

  64 - 4(3 + cos k1)(3 + cos k2), which is why arccos(1/3) appears.

FORMULA 10 — Exact flip-flop Grover-walk trapping on the STAR lattice (3.12.12)

simulation agree.  Novelty: not found in the literature.
==========================================================================================
  The star lattice (truncated hexagonal tiling, each honeycomb vertex replaced by a
  triangle; degree 3) has TWO flat bands of the random walk (mu = 0 and mu = -2/3). So a
  walker is trapped through six channels: +1, -1, +-i, and -2/3 +- i sqrt5/3.
  The Laplacian determinant is proportional to the triangular dispersion, so the
  ordinary (+1) channel and both flat-band channels are RATIONAL. The signless Laplacian
  is the triangular Green function at energy 39 (honeycomb energy 9), which brings in a
  complete elliptic integral with k^2 = 3/128.

  Let  kappa = sqrt(6) K(k) / pi,  k^2 = 3/128   (kappa = 0.7839...)

  start along a triangle edge:
     channels: +1 = 13/200, flat(mu=-2/3) = 79/7200, flat(mu=0) = 1/96,
               -1 = 19/216 - 7 kappa/96 + 3 kappa^2/128
     pbar = 1883/10800 - 7 kappa/96 + 3 kappa^2/128 = 0.1200922563314837020658...
  start along the bridge edge (between triangles):
     channels: +1 = 3/200, flat(mu=-2/3) = 11/1800, flat(mu=0) = 1/72,
               -1 = 1/216 + kappa/48 + 3 kappa^2/128
     pbar = 107/2700 + kappa/48 + 3 kappa^2/128 = 0.1008716548141747862025...
  Signless transfer currents (start along a triangle edge): ((40 + 9 kappa)/72,

  Verification: grid diagonalisation (70x70): 0.1200909-0.1200962 and 0.1008777; the

  Not closed-form (recorded numerically in NOTES.md): ruby 3.4.6.4 (pbar = 0.16458341395062199972

FORMULA 11 — Flip-flop Grover walk on the PYROCHLORE lattice (3D, frustrated-magnet
             lattice): exact ordinary and flat-band trapping channels
             + corollary for edge-transitive bipartite lattices (honeycomb, diamond)
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: 3D exact engine (lattices3d/gen3d.py, validated on simple cubic
to 12 digits) + PSLQ + symbolic assembly; independent 3D Bloch-grid check.
Novelty: not found in the literature.
==========================================================================================
  Pyrochlore = line graph of diamond (corner-sharing tetrahedra), degree 6. Its random walk
  has a DOUBLY DEGENERATE flat band mu = -1/3, so the walker is trapped through channels
  +1, -1 and lambda = -1/3 +- i 2sqrt2/3. The flat-band projector is written exactly as
  F = I - B^dag (B B^dag)^{-1} B with B the diamond incidence matrix (B^dag B = A + 2),
  so everything except the -1 channel reduces to the FCC lattice Green function.

  Let W = W_f = 3 Gamma(1/3)^6 / (2^(14/3) pi^4)  (FCC Watson constant),  X = 1/(pi^2 W).
  Transfer currents from an edge at the origin (Laplacian):
     start edge 1/3; the other two edges of the same tetrahedron 1/6 each (exact);
     edges of the other tetrahedron: (1 + 8W + 3X)/48 and (15 - 8W - 3X)/96 (x2).
  Flat-band projector values: F(o,o) = 1/2, F(o,h) = -1/6,
     F(h_b,h_a) = 1/2, -1/6, -1/6, (8W + 3X - 4)/6, (5 - 8W - 3X)/12 (x2).
  Channel contributions (start on any edge; all edges are equivalent):
     +1   : W^2/96 + WX/128 - 13W/1152 + 3X^2/2048 - 13X/3072 + 2531/18432
          = 0.1342600434822144...
     flat : 3W^2/64 + 9WX/256 - 13W/256 + 27X^2/4096 - 39X/2048 + 163/4096   (both lambdas)
          = 0.0260451956699648...
     -1   : closed form (added later in the session). Let g(t) = E_u[1/(t - s)], s = c1c2 + c2c3 + c3c1
            (the FCC Green function), with g0 = g(15), g1 = g'(15), g2 = g''(15). By Joyce's formula
            g(t) = (1/t) LGF(3/t), LGF(x) = 3^(3/2)(3+x)^(-3/2)(2 - sqrt(1-x)) (2K(k0)/pi)^2,
            k0^2 = 1/2 - (sqrt3/2)(3+x)^(-3/2)(4x + (3-x) sqrt(1-x))  (checked to 30 digits at x = 1/5);
            derivatives follow by the chain rule. The signless transfer currents are exactly
            1/3, (1 - 8g0)/4 (x2), (352g0 + 12672g1 + 92160g2 - 19)/24, (23 - 256g0 - 12672g1 - 92160g2)/48 (x2)
            (PSLQ at 45 digits, integer relations), giving
            -1 = 1907/4608 - 319g0/36 - 671g1/2 - 2440g2 + 70g0^2 + 5280g0g1 + 38400g0g2
                 + 104544g1^2 + 1520640g1g2 + 5529600g2^2
               = 0.13357835580666387432761902262912249...
  TOTAL pbar = 0.29388359496...
  Grid check (40^3 momenta): +1 0.13425969, flat 2 x 0.01302234, -1 0.1335783558066627.
  The gapped -1 channel matches the engine to 15 digits; the others converge toward the
  exact values as the grid is refined (20^3 -> 40^3).

COROLLARY (general): if every edge at a vertex is equivalent (the vertex stabiliser acts
  transitively on its d edges) and the lattice is bipartite and amenable, then the
  transfer currents are forced: Y(e,e) = 2/d (Foster), Y(e,f) = (1 - 2/d)/(d-1). So
      pbar = (d-2)^2 / (2 d (d-1)).
  Honeycomb (d=3): 1/12 (checked, Formula 8); diamond (d=4): 1/6.

FORMULA 24 — A universal lower bound for Grover-walk trapping, exact on "2-arc-symmetric"
             bipartite lattices: pbar >= (d-2)^2 / (2 d (d-1))
Found: 2026-09-27.  Status: proved (short argument below); equality cases checked numerically
by averaging the walk's own eigenprojectors over the Brillouin zone.  Novelty: the ingredients
(Foster's theorem, Cauchy-Schwarz) are classical; the bound and the exact values for diamond,
the Laves graph and d-dimensional diamond were not found.
==========================================================================================
  Setting: flip-flop Grover walk on any periodic graph (any dimension), started on an arc a at a
  vertex o of degree d. As in Formulas 5-23, the long-time average return probability is
     pbar = (1/4) SUM_b (delta_ab - Y_ab)^2 + (1/4) SUM_b (delta_ab - YQ_ab)^2 + (flat-band terms >= 0)
  with Y, YQ the transfer currents of the Laplacian L and signless Laplacian Q.
  FACTS:
   (i)  Rows sum to 1: SUM_b Y_ab = SUM_b YQ_ab = 1 (because SUM_b (e_o - e_hb) = L e_o, SUM_b (e_o + e_hb) = Q e_o).
   (ii) Foster-type trace identities per unit cell: SUM_edges Y_ee = SUM_edges YQ_ee = (vertices per cell)
        (trace of L^-1 L and Q^-1 Q). So on an edge-transitive d-regular lattice Y_aa = YQ_aa = 2/d.
  BOUND (Cauchy-Schwarz on the d - 1 off-diagonal entries, whose sum is 1 - Y_aa by (i)):
     each channel >= (d / (4(d-1))) (1 - Y_aa)^2, so for edge-transitive d-regular lattices
        pbar >= (d - 2)^2 / (2 d (d - 1)).
  EQUALITY holds iff all off-diagonal currents from a are equal in both bands and there are no
  other flat bands. This happens for bipartite lattices whose vertex symmetry permutes the d arcs
  2-transitively (then YQ is Y up to signs):
     honeycomb (d = 3)  pbar = 1/12   (Formula 8)
     Laves graph / K4 crystal / srs net (d = 3, 3D)  pbar = 1/12   (grid: 0.0416667 per channel)
     diamond (d = 4)    pbar = 1/6    (grid 12^3: 0.16650, 16^3: 0.16660)
     d-dimensional diamond (degree D = d + 1): pbar = (d - 1)^2 / (2 d (d + 1));  4D: 9/40 (grid 8^4: 0.22490)
  How tight it is elsewhere (exact values from earlier formulas vs the bound):
     square   0.1673438 vs 1/6       triangular 0.2679557 vs 4/15     simple cubic 0.2667272 vs 4/15
     BCC      0.3215048 vs 9/28      FCC        0.3789146 vs 25/66    (kagome +-1 part 0.1705 vs 1/6)
  So the bound captures every lattice to within about 1e-3, and to within 1e-4 for simple cubic
  and BCC: the off-diagonal transfer currents are nearly equal in practice.
  General form (any start arc, no symmetry): pbar >= (d / (4(d-1))) [ (1 - Y_aa)^2 + (1 - YQ_aa)^2 ].
  (Note: the bipartite equality case was already recorded as a corollary in Formula 11; Formula 24 adds
   the general bound and the Laves-graph and d-dimensional cases.)
  Code: diamond/projgrid3d.py, diamond/srs.py, diamond/hyperdiamond.py

==========================================================================================
  Network: Z^2 with conductance w1 on bonds along e1, w2 along e2, w+ along e1 + e2, w- along e1 - e2.
  Let  sigma = w+ + w-,   Delta = 4 w+ w- - w1^2.  Then
     R(e1 bond) = (2/(pi sqrt(Delta))) arcsinh( sqrt( Delta / ((w1 + sigma)(w1 + w2)) ) )        (Delta > 0)
                = (2/(pi sqrt(-Delta))) arcsin( sqrt( -Delta / ((w1 + sigma)(w1 + w2)) ) )      (Delta < 0)
                = 2 / (pi sqrt((w1 + sigma)(w1 + w2)))                                          (Delta = 0)

     w1 R(e1) + w2 R(e2) + w+ R(e1+e2) + w- R(e1-e2) = 1
